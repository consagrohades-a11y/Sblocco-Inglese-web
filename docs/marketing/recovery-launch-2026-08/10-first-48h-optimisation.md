# Recupero Debito Inglese — First 48 Hours Optimisation

## Objective

The first 48 hours after H30 are for **finding the weakest part of the funnel and validating willingness to pay €39**.

They are not for scaling reach.

Do not import universal conversion benchmarks. The first real launch traffic creates the baseline.

---

# 1. Funnel to measure

Track in this order:

1. **landing visits**
2. **diagnostic started**
3. **diagnostic completed**
4. **product CTA clicked**
5. **signup**
6. **checkout started**
7. **purchase**
8. **onboarding completed**
9. **first session started**
10. **first session completed**

The point is not only to find buyers. It is to confirm that buyers can become active learners.

---

# 2. Diagnosis table

| Observed pattern | Likely problem to investigate first | Marketing action |
|---|---|---|
| Many landing visits, few diagnostic starts | Hook/landing mismatch, unclear value, weak first CTA | Tighten first screen around “Non ripassare tutto a caso”; make free-test time/value visible; compare source message to landing message |
| Many diagnostic starts, weak completion | Test expectation or UX friction | Check mobile flow, load/error behaviour and whether “24 domande · 6–8 minuti” was clear before start; do not solve by adding more ad spend |
| Diagnostic completion is healthy but product CTA clicks are weak | Result → offer transition | Use “Trasforma queste priorità in un piano”; connect the user's result to programme + date + plan before showing price |
| Product CTA clicks but few signups | Account step/friction or weak reason to create account | Clarify why the account is needed and preserve the diagnostic/result context; do not hide the €39 model |
| Signups but few checkout starts | Offer/commerce handoff | Re-check price presentation, one-time/no-subscription reassurance and checkout CTA; verify technical handoff |
| Many checkout starts, no/few purchases | Price/trust/payment/checkout issue | Treat as a lower-funnel/commerce investigation; pause paid purchase traffic if checkout is failing |
| Purchases but onboarding not completed | Post-purchase UX | Do not scale acquisition into an activation leak; escalate onboarding friction to product/technical owners |
| Onboarding completes but first session does not start | Plan reveal / next-step clarity | Strengthen the post-plan CTA and make the first priority obvious |
| First session starts but completion is weak | Session experience / length / task clarity | Do not rewrite acquisition copy first; product experience is now the bottleneck |

# 3. Source-specific diagnosis

## Facebook groups / organic

### Signal to look for

- diagnostic starts/completions per group UTM;
- questions about what the test/product actually is;
- parent versus student language differences;
- product CTA clicks after the result.

### Actions

**Comments/reactions but few diagnostic visits**  
The post may be interesting but the CTA/link is too buried. Move the free-test sentence earlier in the next group variant.

**Visits but few test starts**  
The post may be overpromising versus the diagnostic page or sending curiosity traffic. Tighten the wording around “24 domande / punto di partenza”.

**One group produces relevant completions**  
Do not repost immediately in the same group. Find similar groups and reuse the angle, not the exact spam pattern.

**People ask for tutoring**  
Do not pretend the digital product is automatically a replacement. Position the diagnostic as useful before or alongside tutoring.

---

## Google Search

### Signal to look for

- actual search terms;
- relevance of query to debt/recovery exam;
- diagnostic starts/completions;
- product CTA/checkout movement.

### Actions

**Irrelevant search term**  
Negative it immediately when clearly outside scope.

**Relevant term, no diagnostic start**  
Check ad-to-landing message continuity before changing bids.

**Relevant term produces diagnostic completions but no product clicks**  
Keep the term provisionally; the weakness is lower in the funnel.

**Exact/phrase term produces checkout/purchase movement**  
Keep it active at the current budget. Do not broaden match types during the first 48 hours.

### 48h spend rule

At €9/day, the first Search window is **up to €18**.

Do not increase it during the first 48 hours.

---

## Meta parent test

Meta is optional/secondary during this validation.

### Signal to look for

- adult landing visits;
- diagnostic starts/completions;
- product CTA movement;
- comments that show whether parents understand the offer.

### 48h spend rule

At €6/day, the first Meta window is **up to €12**.

If Search + organic have not produced any meaningful lower-funnel signal, do not use Meta to buy a larger sample just to make the dashboard busier.

If the full €12 test yields no useful diagnostic or product-interest signal, pause and retain the stronger channel(s).

# 4. First 48 hours by time block

## T+0 to T+6 hours

### Do

- confirm every acquisition URL resolves;
- confirm UTMs are appearing in the launch sheet/analytics where available;
- monitor diagnostic errors;
- review Google search terms as soon as data exists;
- answer real organic questions;
- confirm purchases, if any, reach onboarding.

### Do not

- redesign the whole landing page after five visits;
- increase budgets;
- add broad match;
- create new audiences because one ad is quiet;
- declare the product validated from one purchase.

---

## T+6 to T+24 hours

Identify the **single weakest measurable transition**.

Examples:

- visits → diagnostic start;
- diagnostic complete → product CTA;
- checkout start → purchase.

Make **one material change at that stage**, then keep the rest of the funnel stable enough to learn.

### Possible marketing changes

- hook/order of copy;
- result-to-offer CTA language;
- FAQ/trust microcopy placement;
- Search negatives;
- which organic post angle is reused;
- pause one weak Meta concept.

Do not “optimise” a technical failure with copy.

---

## T+24 to T+48 hours

Break performance down by:

- source;
- medium;
- UTM content/creative;
- Google ad group/search term;
- parent vs student organic angle.

Then classify every channel/content cell:

### KEEP

Produces relevant diagnostic or lower-funnel activity and no material trust problem.

### FIX

Produces relevant traffic but stalls at a specific transition that can be changed.

### PAUSE

Produces irrelevant traffic, causes misunderstanding, or feeds a broken lower funnel.

### EXPAND LATER

Produces purchases **and** healthy onboarding/session activation. Do not expand during the first 48h solely because this label exists.

# 5. Decision rules by funnel stage

These rules use **relative behaviour and business guardrails**, not external benchmarks.

## Landing → diagnostic start

If one source has materially worse start behaviour than the others, inspect its message/intent first.

If **all** sources send visits but almost nobody starts, the problem is probably not the channel. Fix the landing proposition/CTA or verify the diagnostic experience.

## Diagnostic start → complete

If completion is weak across all sources, check product/test friction.

If completion is weak only from one source, that source may be sending users who expected something different.

## Diagnostic complete → product CTA

This is the key test of whether the free value creates paid-product interest.

If users finish the test but do not want to see the path, strengthen the bridge:

> “Hai le priorità. Ora aggiungi programma e data della prova per trasformarle in un piano.”

Do not simply make the buy button louder.

## Product CTA → signup → checkout

If users express product intent but stop around account/checkout, preserve the diagnostic context and keep price/payment model clear.

Do not hide that the product costs €39 until the last step.

## Checkout → purchase

A checkout start is strong intent. If purchase fails disproportionately here, investigate commerce/trust/payment before changing the top-of-funnel ads.

## Purchase → onboarding → first session

This is the activation test.

A sale is not a healthy launch if the buyer cannot start the recovery path.

# 6. Paid spend and scaling guardrails

## First 48h maximum if both channels are activated

- Google Search: **€18**
- Meta: **€12**
- Combined: **€30**

This is an operating cap for the initial test, not a claim about required spend.

## CAC interpretation

Compare observed CAC against the hypotheses in `02-offer.md`:

- €10 conservative target;
- €15 acceptable validation tolerance;
- €20 hard experimental ceiling for a very small sample.

These are internal business choices, not industry averages.

## Hard pause concept

If cumulative paid spend later reaches **€39 (one full product price) with zero checkout starts**, pause paid acquisition and diagnose the funnel before spending another equivalent amount.

If checkout starts exist but purchase does not, the problem may be lower-funnel and paid intent should not automatically be blamed.

## Scaling gate

Do **not** scale because of:

- impressions;
- clicks;
- likes;
- diagnostic starts alone;
- one purchase.

Consider scaling only when there are **multiple purchases plus working onboarding and first-session activation**.

A practical validation gate is to wait for at least **3 real purchases** before treating CAC as more than a single-customer anecdote. This is an internal launch rule, not a statistical or market benchmark.

# 8. What counts as launch validation

## Weak signal

- traffic;
- likes/comments;
- people taking a free test without moving further.

Useful for copy learning, but not willingness-to-pay validation.

## Medium signal

- diagnostic completions;
- product CTA clicks;
- signups;
- checkout starts.

Shows increasing intent but still not revenue validation.

## Strong commercial signal

- real €39 purchases from people outside the existing team/test accounts.

## Strong product signal

- purchasers complete onboarding;
- purchasers begin and complete the first recovery session.

The launch succeeds first by proving **real purchase intent and usable activation**, then by improving acquisition efficiency.
